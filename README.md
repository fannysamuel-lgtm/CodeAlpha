🔬 Task 1: Protein Sequence Analysis using BLAST

🎯 Objective

To analyze a protein sequence using BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool) and identify similar protein sequences from biological databases.

🛠️ Tool Used

- NCBI BLAST
- BLASTp (Protein–Protein BLAST)

🧪 Methodology

1. Obtained the protein sequence for analysis.
2. Submitted the sequence to the NCBI BLASTp tool.
3. Compared the query sequence against the protein database.
4. Analyzed the alignment results, including percentage identity, query coverage, and E-value.

📊 Results

- Protein: Insulin, isoform 2 precursor
- Organism: Homo sapiens
- Accession: NP_001035835.1
- Query Length: 200 amino acids
- Database: ClusteredNR

🔍 BLAST Results

Parameter| Result
Maximum Identity| 100%
Query Coverage| 100%
E-value| 2e-140
Maximum Score| 400

🧠 Interpretation

The BLAST results showed a highly significant match with 100% sequence identity and 100% query coverage. The extremely low E-value indicates that the alignment is statistically significant and unlikely to occur by chance.

This analysis confirms the identity and similarity of the query protein sequence with the matching sequence in the database.

🧬 Task 2: Multiple Sequence Alignment

🎯 Objective

To compare protein sequences from different organisms and identify conserved and variable regions through multiple sequence alignment.

🛠️ Tool Used

- Clustal Omega

🧪 Methodology

1. Collected insulin protein sequences from different organisms.
2. Submitted the sequences to Clustal Omega.
3. Performed multiple sequence alignment.
4. Examined conserved residues and sequence variations.
5. Generated the alignment output in Clustal format.

🧬 Sequences Compared

Protein sequences of insulin from different organisms were compared to study evolutionary conservation.

📊 Results

The multiple sequence alignment revealed conserved regions across the selected insulin sequences, along with variations at certain positions.

🔍 Key Observations

- Conserved amino acid positions indicate evolutionary similarity.
- Sequence variations may reflect species-specific differences.
- Alignment helps identify similarities in protein structure and function.
- Conserved regions may be important for biological activity.

🧠 Interpretation

Multiple sequence alignment is useful for studying evolutionary relationships among proteins. Conserved regions in insulin sequences suggest that the protein has maintained important structural and functional characteristics across species.

🧫 Task 3: Protein Structure Prediction and Visualization

🎯 Objective

To explore the three-dimensional structure of a protein using bioinformatics resources and visualize its structural features.

🛠️ Tools Used

- AlphaFold Protein Structure Database
- Mol Molecular Visualization Tool*
- UniProt

🧪 Methodology

1. Identified the insulin protein sequence using UniProt.
2. Explored the available protein structure information.
3. Retrieved the structure file, if available.
4. Opened the protein structure using Mol* Viewer.
5. Visualized the protein in 3D using cartoon representation.
6. Rotated and zoomed the structure to examine its structural organization.

🧬 Protein Information

- Protein: Insulin
- Organism: Homo sapiens
- UniProt Accession: P01308

📊 Results

The protein structure was visualized in three-dimensional space to explore its structural organization and folding.

🔍 Key Observations

- Protein structures can be examined in three dimensions.
- Structural visualization helps understand protein folding.
- Different structural representations help identify structural features.
- 3D visualization provides insights beyond primary amino acid sequence analysis.

🧠 Interpretation

Protein structure visualization provides an understanding of the spatial organization of amino acids and the relationship between protein structure and function.
